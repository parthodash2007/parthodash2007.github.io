Topic Overview

01 Physical Quantity

A physical quantity is a property of a physical object or phenomenon that can be measured and expressed numerically with a unit.

Examples

Length = 5 metre, Mass = 2 kilogram, Time = 10 second.

02 Fundamental and Derived Quantities

Fundamental quantities are independent quantities that are not defined in terms of other physical quantities. Derived quantities are expressed using fundamental quantities.

Quantity Type Example SI Unit Symbol
Length Fundamental metre m
Mass Fundamental kilogram kg
Time Fundamental second s
Velocity Derived metre per second m s⁻¹
Force Derived newton N

03 SI System of Units

The International System of Units (SI) is the internationally accepted system of measurement. It has seven base units.

Base Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length metre m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Electric current ampere A
Thermodynamic temperature kelvin K
Amount of substance mole mol
Luminous intensity candela cd

04 Dimensions of Physical Quantities

Dimensions represent how a physical quantity depends on fundamental quantities such as mass, length and time.

Dimensional Formula
[MaLbTc]

Here, M represents mass, L represents length, and T represents time.

Quantity Dimensional Formula
Area [L²]
Velocity [LT⁻¹]
Acceleration [LT⁻²]
Force [MLT⁻²]
Work [ML²T⁻²]
Pressure [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

05 Dimensional Analysis

Dimensional analysis is a method of examining physical relationships using the dimensions of physical quantities.

Uses of dimensional analysis

  • Checking the dimensional consistency of equations.
  • Deriving possible relationships between quantities.
  • Converting units from one system to another.
Important

Dimensional consistency is necessary for a physically meaningful equation, but it does not prove that an equation is completely correct.
